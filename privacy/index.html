Privacy

Last updated 10 September 2026 · app.beagle.chat

Beagle Web is a peer in a browser tab. There is no account to create and no server holding your messages, because there is nowhere for one to be held — your key is generated in the tab and stays there. This page says exactly what is kept on your device, what leaves it, and who can see what.

  • Your private key is generated in your browser and never sent anywhere.
  • Message and file contents are encrypted end to end; the servers in between carry bytes they cannot read.
  • There is no analytics, no tracking script and no advertising in this app, and it sets no cookies.
  • What is unavoidably visible is metadata — your IP address, and the fact that you connected. Section 3 is specific about it.

01 What stays on your device

All of it lives in your browser's own storage for this site, in IndexedDB — or in localStorage where IndexedDB cannot be used, which is the case in Safari. None of it is uploaded.

  • Your identity — the keypair that is your account.
  • Your profile — display name, one-line description, chosen avatar.
  • Your friends and the state your peer keeps about them.
  • Your message history, and files you have sent or received.

Clearing this site's data destroys the identity. Nothing can reissue it — no one holds a copy, which is the same property that keeps it private. Use the key backup in the app to export it before you clear anything, and note that a private window discards everything when you close it.

02 What does not exist

  • No account, no email address, no password, no phone number.
  • No server-side copy of your messages, your contacts or your files.
  • No analytics, telemetry, session recording or A/B tooling.
  • No cookies set by this app, and no advertising or data brokers.
  • No profile sold, shared or handed to anyone, because none is collected.

03 What travels, and who sees it

Message and file content

Encrypted between you and the friend you are talking to. A browser tab cannot open a raw network socket, so traffic goes through a WebSocket bridge to the peer-to-peer relays. The bridge and the relays forward encrypted bytes and cannot read them.

Metadata, which encryption does not hide

Any server you connect to sees your IP address and the time you connected. That is true of the bridge, the relays, and every host in the table below.

What the app tells the bridge

On connecting, it announces your public key (your user id) and your Carrier address — these are what let anyone reach you at all. Before you create an identity, on a first visit, it announces nothing.

While “Let people on this bridge see you” is on, it also sends your display name, description and avatar id, and you appear in the bridge's list of people present, so someone with no friends yet can find you. Turning it off in Profile tells the bridge not to list you. It is your choice, it can be changed at any time, and it changes nothing about who can message you.

Calls and browser-to-browser file transfer

These use WebRTC, which needs STUN servers to discover how to connect. When a direct connection cannot be made, the stream is relayed by a TURN server. Both see your IP address; the relayed stream stays encrypted and the relay cannot read it.

05 What the people you talk to see

Your display name, description and avatar, and whatever you send them. They hold their own copy of your conversation on their own device, and what they do with it is up to them — as with any messenger, and as with any conversation.

06 Changes

This page is versioned alongside the code it describes, so its history is the record of what changed and when. If the app starts doing something this page does not say, treat that as a bug in the app or in this page, and one worth reporting.
